Intro script

About 75 seconds read aloud. Each paragraph follows one chapter of screenshots below, in order.

Every software company hits the same wall: one developer, and a hundred things to build. RunHQ removes that wall.

RunHQ is a workspace where your whole team ships with Claude Code. Start as many jobs as you want, side by side. Every job is the real Claude Code, on your own Claude account, working on its own branch. Read along in a clean, formatted view, or switch to the terminal and you’re in the same session.

When a job is done, open the preview and see the change running in your app before it gets anywhere near production. Every job opens a pull request, gets audited and ships with a deploy, and the tickets it closes are marked shipped.

Your customers are part of the loop. The RunHQ widget lives inside your product. People report bugs, ask for features and vote on what matters. Each ticket arrives with the page, the browser and the errors they hit, ready to hand to a job.

Those customers are your business, so RunHQ includes a full CRM. Chat, email, SMS, WhatsApp and calls all land in one inbox. Every account and every person comes with their history, plus automations, campaigns, and an AI support agent that answers for you.

Invite your teammates. Product, support and sales can all file work and start jobs, so one developer is no longer the bottleneck for everything.

Then let it evolve. Give RunHQ a goal, like more sign-ups or more teams inviting a colleague. Its agents work out how to measure it, read what your users are saying, and propose variations. They run those variations side by side on real traffic and keep the winner, generation after generation.

The homepage: now, and what to change

Checked on 2026-10-29 at desktop (1440px) and mobile (390px) widths. The page served at / is HomePage.tsx in the runhq-io/homepage repo.

How it is now

  • Hero: the headline “Make your company self‑evolve.” has no subheading and no button. The only call to action on the page is “Contact sales” in the top bar. A subheading (“RunHQ builds versions of your site, tests them on real visitors, and keeps the ones that convert.”) and “Start free / Book a demo” buttons exist only as A/B test versions, and a normal visit doesn’t get them.
  • The whole page is five blocks: the nav, the hero, a logo strip, “Evolution, automated.” with an animated bar chart of checkout variants, and a black closing band (“Build a company that runs itself.”, also without a button). Then the footer. On desktop that is about 2,700px.
  • No product anywhere. The hero is a faint pixel blob and a giant “RunHQ” wordmark that runs off the bottom of the screen. There are no screenshots of jobs, Claude Code, the preview, the widget, the CRM or the team.
  • It only tells the Evolve story, and only as website A/B testing. Parallel Claude Code jobs, preview, feedback from the widget, the CRM and teammates are all missing, so a visitor can’t tell it is a development product.
  • Mobile: the nav wraps onto two lines, and the headline sits about 470px down, under mostly empty space. The floating “HQ” widget tab covers the “R” of the wordmark (on desktop too). The closing line cross-fades, so it can be captured half-blurred.
  • Sharing and search: the page renders in the browser, and its index.html has only a <title>. There is no meta description and no Open Graph or Twitter image, so a shared link, including the video’s, previews as a bare URL.
  • Stale page: /products (not linked from the nav) still pitches dispatching to “Claude Code, Cursor, Codex, or Devin”.
  • What works: it is fast (about 335KB, no console errors), the type is confident, and the evolving-variants chart is the page’s best idea.

What to change, most important first

  1. Say what it is in the first screen. Keep “self‑evolve” as the payoff, but put a concrete subheading and two buttons under it. For example:
    Make your company self‑evolve.
    Run Claude Code jobs in parallel, each on its own branch with a live preview. Your team files the work, your customers’ feedback becomes tickets, and Evolve keeps whatever moves your numbers.
    Start free · Book a demo
  2. Replace the blob and wordmark with the product. Use a 10–15 second muted loop cut from this video: the job list with several jobs running, one opened to Claude Code working, then the preview. Good stills are 01-01, 01-02 and 02-01 above.
  3. Add one section per pillar, each with a real screenshot, in the same order as the video. Each chapter on this page is ready to use as a section:
    1. Parallel jobs on the real Claude Code
    2. Preview, then ship
    3. Feedback straight from users (widget)
    4. A full CRM
    5. Your whole team ships
    6. Evolve, keeping the variant chart as the hero image of this section, reworded for any goal rather than only checkout
    That order moves from something every developer recognises to the big vision, where today’s page starts at the vision and stops there.
  4. Put a button at the end of every version. Make the closing_ctas version the default, and hold each frame of the closing line long enough that it never shows half-blurred.
  5. Fix mobile.
    • Put the brand, “Start free” and a menu on one row.
    • Move the headline into the top third, and keep the subheading and button in the first screen.
    • Move the widget tab off the wordmark.
  6. Back up the logos. Add one named quote or number next to the logo strip, such as jobs shipped per week or a conversion lift from Evolve. If not all of those companies use Evolve, change “Companies evolving on RunHQ” to “Teams building on RunHQ”.
  7. Clean up around the page.
    • Add a meta description and an Open Graph image, for example a still from the video.
    • Pre-render / so search engines and link previews get the content.
    • Rewrite or retire /products, and retire the hidden /quiet headline experiments.
    • Make “Start free” the main button in the top bar. “Contact sales” alone reads as enterprise-only.
